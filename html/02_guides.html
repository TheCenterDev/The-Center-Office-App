Apps

Credit Card Reconciliation

Download the Expensify report and the Credit Union deposit PDF, then open the tool and follow the on-screen steps. It compares the two reports and matches them up for you automatically. From there, you're ready to finish the rest of the reconciliation.

Whitley County Resource Directory

A searchable, filterable directory of local resources for families and staff — childcare, housing, counseling, food assistance, financial help, education/career, and emergency services — pulled from the Whitley County Resource Guide. Faster than flipping through the PDF mid-conversation.

New Hire Onboarding Tracker

A checklist for onboarding a new hire, grounded in the Employee Handbook — accounts and access, benefits and pay, policies, and orientation. Tracks progress per hire and can email a progress update straight to the directors with one click. Everyone sees the same hires and the same ticked boxes, on any device.

Admins and Directors can change the checklist itself with Edit Checklist: add, rename, or remove sections and items. Changes apply to every hire, current and future. Rewording an item keeps whoever had already ticked it ticked. You can also add items that apply to one hire only, under Additional Items.

Building Maintenance Log

Log a facility issue with location, priority, and a description, then track it through Open → In Progress → Done. Everyone signed in sees the same list, so a request logged on a phone at Busco shows up on the office computer. Export to CSV any time for a board update or contractor conversation.

Each new request also becomes a task in the Todoist Facilities project automatically, usually within about five minutes. Urgent items arrive as priority 1.

It works in both directions. Tick the task off in Todoist and the entry here moves to Done with the date it was completed. Mark it Done here and the Todoist task closes. If someone deletes a Todoist task rather than completing it, the request deliberately stays open and says so in red — deleting usually means it shouldn't have been posted, not that the work is finished, and quietly closing a real repair on that basis would lose it.

Calendar

Dates for yourself, for specific people, or for the whole team, in a month grid you can click through. Set how far ahead you want warning — on the day, three days, a week — and when that point arrives, the date appears as a reminder across the top of the app and the mobile site until you dismiss it.

Email addresses, phone numbers and web links typed into a date's notes become clickable, so you can go straight from the calendar to the message or the meeting. To add a lot of dates at once, open Upload a lot of dates at once, take a copy of the linked Google Sheets template, fill it in, and download it as CSV — the tool shows you a preview and flags any bad rows before anything is saved. A PDF works too, but it has to be reconstructed from the page, so check the preview carefully.

Who sees a date works the same way as Notes: "just me" is hidden from everyone including Directors, "the whole team" is visible to everyone signed in, and "specific people" is visible only to whoever you name. The recurring reporting deadlines below also appear on the grid, marked in amber, so what's coming up is answerable in one place — but they're still edited in the Reporting Calendar.

Reporting Calendar

Every recurring reporting obligation in one place — the monthly JDAI report, survey windows, year-end close-out — with what's coming up in the next 60 days and a full year view. Deadlines turn into Todoist tasks about ten days ahead, so nothing depends on someone remembering.

Admins and Directors can edit it. An obligation that ends can be switched off rather than deleted, which keeps the record in case it comes back — that's how the 21st CCLC cycle is stored now that the grant has finished.

Notes

Three kinds of note in one place: notes just for you, notes for the whole team, and notes left for specific people. Anything left for you shows up under For me marked unread. Notes follow you between the office computer and your phone.

"Just me" notes are hidden from everyone else, including Directors. They are not hidden from whoever administers the database, so treat them as work scratch space rather than somewhere for anything genuinely confidential.

Count Log

A big tap counter for anything you count by hand — headcount, snacks and meals served, volunteers, van riders. Tap it or just type the number, say what and where in your own words, add a note, and save. It remembers what's already been logged for that day so two people don't double-count the same thing.

Totals add themselves up by category and site over any date range, and export to CSV. This is where the snack and meal numbers reported to Parkview come from, so they're counted rather than estimated.

Program Timer

A big countdown for activity blocks, with presets for homework help, activity, snack and clean-up. Build a station rotation and it chimes and moves to the next station on its own. No sign-in, nothing saved, and it works without internet — prop a phone on a shelf and let it run the room.

Skills

Skills are things Claude already knows how to do for this office — just tell Claude what you need in plain language, no app to open. To use one outside this launcher (in your own Claude or Cowork chat), download it below and attach the file to a conversation. Once it's attached, type / followed by the skill's name (for example, /deposit-reconciliation) to run it directly — or just describe what you need and Claude will use it on its own when it fits.

Center Context

Not a task — this one just gives Claude the background it would otherwise ask you for every time: our sites and programs, who does what, what JDAI and ASC and Ground Zero mean, which grants are active, and how the Office App and its database fit together. Install it once and Claude stops needing the explanation. Download Skill

It's meant to be kept up to date by anyone. If something in it is wrong or has changed, just say so — "update the Center context skill, Isaiah moved to the Busco site" — and Claude will edit it. Worth doing whenever someone joins or leaves, a grant starts or ends, or a tool changes. One caveat: everyone has their own copy, so after a real change the file should be shared round again.

Try asking:

  • "What's due for JDAI this month?"
  • "Who should I ask about a payroll question?"
  • "How do I push a change to the app?"
  • "What does ASC/SERVE mean?"

Deposit Reconciliation

Ask Claude to reconcile a bank deposit against the donor deposit-detail export, and it checks that the two actually match before doing anything else. Once they line up, it builds the polished Deposit Detail PDF the board is used to seeing — Checks/Total Deposit, Restricted vs. Unrestricted, and Payment Method breakdowns. If something's off, it stops and tells you exactly what doesn't match instead of guessing. Download Skill

Try asking:

  • /deposit-reconciliation
  • "Reconcile this deposit against QuickBooks."
  • "Here's the donor export and the QuickBooks deposit — do they match?"
  • "Put together the deposit detail PDF for this batch."

Financial Summary Email

Ask Claude to draft the monthly or year-to-date budget-vs-actual financial summary email, and it fills in every one of the organization's income and expense categories — never skipping one, even if a number is missing for it. It'll check with you for a quick explanation on anything newly over budget before finishing the draft. Download Skill

Try asking:

  • /financial-summary-email
  • "Draft this month's financials email."
  • "Compile the budget vs actual email with these QuickBooks numbers."
  • "Write the monthly financial summary for the board."
